# Curated Detail Hierarchy Rule (v59)

Dense detail must be authored, not scattered.

V11 proved that a technically valid dense city can still read as random slop if props are evenly distributed without hierarchy. The fix is not simply fewer parts; it is clearer design structure.

## Detail hierarchy

1. Macro silhouette.
2. Primary walkable paths and structural rhythm.
3. District or zone identity.
4. Authored modules: shop, kiosk, service bay, cafe, cargo dock, garden pocket, shrine, lab station.
5. Secondary props attached to those modules.
6. Final accent clutter only where it reinforces the scene.

## Practical rules

- Preserve clear walking lanes.
- Move pipes/utilities to service zones, walls, overhead runs, underside spaces, or perimeter bands.
- Group props into small readable stories.
- Use repetition with variation, not identical scatter.
- Keep negative space so close-up screenshots have composition.

This rule applies to any future dense build, not just megacities.
## recognizability levers and the finish lever

Three levers make a build read as a real object, chosen by budget:
- Low-budget silhouette variety (#15): few parts, many distinct types (~54% unique); a handful of signature silhouette parts carry recognition, cheap interior variety fills it out.
- Canonical proportions + standard feature placement + per-instance color (#18): identical parts differentiated by color plus correct proportions and standard feature positions (e.g., pool table 2:1 bed, regulation 6-pocket layout, 15-color ball set).
- Hyper-dense focal showpiece (#10): high count, low type variety; reserve for one focal accent.

Finish lever — gap-closing densification (#19): the solidity/smoothness of a segmented or faceted assembly (rings, faceted walls) is controlled by part COUNT at a fixed envelope and fixed part scale, not by enlarging parts. To close gaps / read solid, add segments (measured: a portal ring 12 -> 32 segments on the identical circle, envelope unchanged). Cheap while under the ~4-5k flicker ceiling.
